# Quick research

Goal: in about 5–10 minutes, check the first look's bets against public evidence and come back with the same step, revised. This is not a research report: it exists to change (or confirm) what gets built. Do it yourself, in this conversation, with WebSearch and WebFetch.

The first look is your page-only look. Everything you find is measured against it.

## What to look at

The surface from phase 1 decides the checks: a **website page** (a landing or home page, where visitors decide to sign up, download or buy) or a **product step** (onboarding, sign-up, a paywall, an upgrade, inside the product). Run only what the signals call for (phase 1 §4), in one batch of parallel searches and fetches — about ten fetches in all, public pages only:

1. **What they can truthfully claim** — the first look already read the surface; add the live pages it didn't read: numbers, prices, guarantees, proof, word for word, with the URL. These are the only claims the options may use.
   - *Website page:* the pages around it (pricing, FAQ, about).
   - *Product step:* the pricing and plan pages — what's free, the limits, what each plan unlocks.
2. **Where paid traffic lands** — website pages only, and only when there are ads signals. The sitemap, campaign-style landing paths, and the Meta Ad Library and Google Ads Transparency Center for the domain, each fetched once; a library that comes back unreadable goes in the brief as skipped. If paid traffic lands somewhere other than the surface you chose, that's the headline finding: say so and propose the right surface.
3. **Competitors** — three to five, from the category in their own words ("<category> alternatives", their comparison pages).
   - *Website page:* each one's hero claim, and what nobody claims — an open angle is a strong option.
   - *Product step:* how they run the same step and where their choices differ from this one, from their public docs, help pages and pricing.
4. **Customer voice** — reviews and threads (G2, Product Hunt, app stores, Reddit, Hacker News): the words people use, and what stops them **at or before** this surface.
   - *Website page:* what holds visitors back from signing up or buying.
   - *Product step:* what people say about that step, in their own words.

No logins, no forms; skip bot walls and captchas rather than working around them. Note the date of anything time-sensitive (prices, rates, ads).

**The conversion filter:** keep a finding only if it bears on whether a visitor converts on this surface. Everything else is noise for now.

## What comes back

Write `.levered/research/brief.md`: the sources with dates, the claims, the competitor claims and open angles, the customers' words, what changed, and **what you skipped and why**, a line each. It's the evidence later check-ins and new ideas draw on.

Then one short message — the same step again, revised:

- Each problem or change **held, stronger or weaker**, with the one piece of evidence that moved it, in a clause.
- Sharper "e.g." copy where the research found a true claim or the customers' own words.
- A new one only on strong evidence; drop one that the evidence undercuts.
- One line on what the research changed — or, plainly, that it changed nothing, which means the page-only look was enough. Anything else worth knowing is a clause or a new item, not a paragraph.

End with the same question, without the option they just used — keep any strengthen option they haven't tried. Never invent a claim, a number or a quote: if you can't point to the source, it doesn't go in.
